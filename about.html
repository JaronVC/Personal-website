why i'm doing this:

silly attempt at a serious explanation: I like it.

I love the indie web and I love to get to call a little space of the world wide web mine

I notice I'm often having the same discussions about the same topics in philosophy. I figured it would be useful to actually think about everything I believe, write down all the reaons for why I believe them and then be able to direct people to this to actually have conversations go further.

silly attempt at poetry: Maybe I shouldn't dissect everything so much. Maybe things fall apart when you dissect them too much. But even this proposition about dissecting leaves me little room not to dissect. It seems falsifiable, examinable, makeable, breakable and most importantly thinkable. It makes you think can it make you think it can it does and it will, these thinks happen. Think about how hard it is to leave thought alone when thought is telling you to leave thought alone.

And why oh why shouldn't I oh I? I am a curious creature. I'd like to know what things are, what makes them such and such and so and so, whether they could be otherwise, what makes them what and whether the answers I find are answers at all.

Of course, life is not meant to be anything. To say that it was not meant to be understood would be no less presumptuous than to say that it was. There is no obvious purpose against which I can measure my own. And yet the absence of a predetermined purpose does not make the question disappear. It merely deprives me of an answer I could have accepted without having to explain why I accept it, which is probably for the better since I wouldn't accept it either way.

I have come too far to return to the unexamined life, or perhaps I have simply become accustomed to examining it. Having questioned a little, I feel compelled to question further. But whys should I question more if I've already questioned before? Why should I question why to question?

Other people seem to find ways of living without an equal amount of questioning. Their lives need not be any less meaningful, considered, or good for it. I have no reason to suppose that a life requires philosophical justification in order to be lived well. But I wonder whether I even want the thing that all this questioning is supposed to help me attain: a good life. I know, at least in broad terms, what human beings tend to need in order to flourish. I can eat properly, exercise, sleep enough, maintain relationships, pursue interests, and organise my circumstances in ways that make my life more "good".

And what is that end? Happiness, perhaps. Wellbeing. Flourishing. The familiar collection of things we call good, as though naming them could settle the question of why they should be pursued. I can understand why a person wants to feel good. I can understand the biological and psychological mechanisms that make certain experiences desirable, the ways in which pleasure reinforces behaviour and suffering discourages it. I can even explain why a creature constituted as I am would generally prefer satisfaction to distress. But an explanation of a desire is not at all a justification of it (at all). To establish that an action promotes my wellbeing is not (at all) to establish that my wellbeing is something that needs to be established.

Perchance. (you can just say perchance) There is nothing further to be found, because the wand simply wants. But then I want to know what follows from this. Why should the fact that an experience is pleasant give me a reason to pursue it, beyond the fact that I happen to desire it? To answer that pleasure is desirable because it feels good seems to bring me back to the very desire whose authority I am trying to understand.

I could spend my life maximizing my wellbeing, arranging my habits, relationships, environment, and thoughts so as to produce the most favourable experiences available to me. I could learn which activities improve my mood, which conditions make me content, which combinations of sleep, exercise, food, and companionship yield the best results. I could become remarkably proficient at living well. But to what end? Am I optimizing myself as a biological system whose purpose is to maintain itself and optimise its own functioning? A collection of matter that has acquired the capacity to experience certain configurations of itself as desirable, and which now organises its existence around reproducing them?

Perchance every attempt to establish an ultimate reason for living will eventually arrive at a premise that cannot itself be justified without presupposing some further value. Perhaps there is no view from nowhere, no standpoint outside desire and experience from which I can determine whether desire and experience are worth having. And with that I would like to continue experiencing and desiring.
